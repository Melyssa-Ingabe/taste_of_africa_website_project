West Africa

Bold, spicy and made for sharing

Food Culture

West African food is lively, social and often eaten with the hands. In Accra, Ghana, Makola Market is the biggest market in the country, packed with fresh fruit, snacks and hot meals sold on the street. Restaurants set out soap and a jar of water so diners can wash their hands before eating, because dishes like banku with okra soup are best enjoyed by hand. Across the region, meals are built around a starchy staple served with a rich soup, stew or fiery pepper sauce.

Staple Foods

  • Cassava
  • Corn (maize)
  • Rice
  • Okra
  • Plantain
  • Beans
  • Eggs
  • Coconut
  • Fresh hot peppers

Cooking Techniques

  • Fermenting: banku is made from a fermented mix of cassava and corn dough, which gives it a slightly tangy taste.
  • Stewing soups: okra soup is cooked with beef and served beside the banku.
  • Deep-frying: grated cassava is mixed with salt and onion, then deep-fried until crispy and eaten with coconut.
  • Boiling and pepper sauce: boiled eggs are sold on the street with a hot fresh pepper sauce.
  • One-pot rice: jollof rice is cooked in a seasoned tomato and pepper sauce so the rice absorbs the flavour.
