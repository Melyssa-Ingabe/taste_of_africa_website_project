West Africa

Bold, spicy and made for sharing

Food Culture

West African cooking is known for its bold, layered flavours and its love of heat. Meals are social: big pots are cooked for families, weddings and festivals, and food is often shared from a common dish. Sauces and stews are built slowly from tomatoes, onions and peppers, then seasoned with scotch bonnet peppers, ginger, garlic, thyme and curry powder. Many dishes are served with a starchy side that is used to scoop up the sauce.

Staple Foods

  • Rice
  • Yams
  • Cassava
  • Plantain
  • Millet and sorghum
  • Fonio
  • Maize
  • Groundnuts
  • Okra
  • Palm oil

Cooking Techniques

  • One-pot rice: rice is cooked directly in a seasoned tomato and pepper sauce, as in jollof, so it absorbs all the flavour.
  • Stewing: rich soups and stews are simmered slowly with palm oil, peppers and leafy greens.
  • Pounding: yam, cassava or plantain is pounded into a smooth, stretchy dough called fufu.
  • Grilling: meat is rubbed with a spicy peanut mix and grilled over fire, as in suya.
  • Smoking and drying: fish and meat are smoked or dried to add depth to soups and sauces.
