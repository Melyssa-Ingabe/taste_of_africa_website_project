About Us

Celebrating African food, one dish at a time

Our Mission

Tastes of Africa exists to promote and preserve African food heritage. We share traditional recipes, the stories behind them and the people who cook them, so that more people can learn about, cook and enjoy the flavours of the continent.

A Brief History of African Cuisine

African cooking is built on ingredients that grew on the continent for thousands of years, such as teff, sorghum, millet, yams, okra and fonio. Each region developed its own methods, from the fermented injera of Ethiopia to the pounded doughs and slow-cooked stews of West and Central Africa.

Trade and travel added new flavours over time. Along the East African coast, trade across the Indian Ocean brought spices and cooking styles from Arabia and India, which is one reason dishes like chapati are loved in Kenya and Tanzania today. Later, crops from the Americas, including maize, cassava, groundnuts, tomatoes and chili peppers, arrived in Africa and became central to everyday meals. Many of the dishes in this website, such as jollof rice and isombe, use them.

African Food Around the World

African foods and cooking traditions have travelled far beyond the continent. Okra, rice dishes and one-pot cooking carried by African people shaped the cuisines of the Caribbean, Brazil and the southern United States. Today, dishes like jollof rice and suya appear on restaurant menus around the world, and ancient grains such as teff and fonio are being rediscovered by chefs and home cooks everywhere.

Visit a Food Landmark: Makola Market

Makola Market in Accra, Ghana, is the biggest market in the country and a lively place to see African food culture first-hand, with stalls of fresh fruit, street snacks and hot meals. It also appears in our West Africa video.
