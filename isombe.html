Isombe

Region: East Africa • Country: Rwanda

Isombe is a much-loved Rwandan dish made from pounded cassava leaves, cooked slowly with leeks, green bell peppers, groundnuts and meat or fish. It is a family meal that is cooked in a big pot and kept warm over several days, and many people say it tastes even better the next day. It is traditionally served with cassava bread (ubugari).

Ingredients

The video does not give exact measurements, so adjust the amounts to the size of your pot and your taste.

For the isombe

  • Cassava leaves, pounded or ground
  • Leeks, ground with the leaves
  • Green bell peppers, ground with the leaves
  • Water or stock (vegetable, chicken, beef or fish, to match the meat you use)
  • Raw meat or fish of your choice
  • Garlic, ground in a mortar
  • Palm oil
  • Isombe seasoning cubes (green, sold in Rwandan markets)
  • Ground groundnuts (peanuts with their skins on)
  • Salt

For the cassava bread

  • Cassava flour
  • Water

Cooking Instructions

Step 1: Prepare the leaves

Traditionally the cassava leaves, leeks and green bell peppers are pounded by hand, which takes a long time. Today many markets have machines that grind them for a small fee, so the mixture comes ready to cook.

Step 2: Dry-fry the leaves

Put the ground mixture in a pan and toss it around for a minute or so until the heat has gone all the way through. This removes the raw, bitter taste of the cassava leaves. The colour will change from bright green to a deeper army green.

Step 3: Add liquid and simmer

Stir and cover, stir and cover, then add water or stock. Use a stock that matches your meat, such as beef stock for beef or fish stock for fish, to keep the flavour consistent. Cover and let it simmer until the liquid has reduced.

Step 4: Add the meat

Because isombe cooks for a long time, the meat goes in raw. At this stage the isombe is not seasoned with salt yet. Cover and keep cooking.

Step 5: Stir often

Stir regularly so the leaves do not burn at the bottom of the pot, especially if you are cooking over charcoal.

Step 6: Add the oil, seasoning and garlic

Once it has reduced and is nearly ready, stir in the palm oil and crumble in the isombe seasoning cubes. Grind the garlic in a mortar, add it, and rinse the mortar with a little water so nothing is wasted. Pour that back into the pot, stir, and bring it to a boil.

Step 7: Add the groundnuts and salt

Mix in the ground groundnuts, using more or less to taste, but balanced. Add the salt now, stir well, and cook on low heat for a few more minutes until the meat is very tender.

Step 8: Make the cassava bread

Bring water to a boil in a pot, add some cassava flour on top, and let the water bubble up around it. Keep adding flour while mixing. Cassava flour does not cook well on high heat, so take the pot off the heat and beat out the lumps firmly with your spoon until the bread is smooth.

Step 9: Serve

Serve the isombe hot with the cassava bread. Isombe keeps well: it can be warmed up over several days and many people find it tastes even better the next day.
